## Purpose

Pattern Intelligence transforms independent market observations into verified behavioural knowledge.

Individual observations describe events.

Patterns explain why those events repeatedly occur.

The objective is discovering repeatable success mechanisms.

---

## Mission

Pattern Intelligence answers five questions.

1. Which observations repeat?
2. Which observations are independent?
3. Which observations form consistent behavioural patterns?
4. Can those patterns be verified?
5. Can they predict future opportunity?

---

## Pattern Definition

A Pattern represents a repeatable behavioural relationship supported by multiple independent observations.

Patterns never originate from isolated success.

Patterns require evidence.

---

## Pattern Categories

Pattern Intelligence may identify:

- Search Patterns
- Content Patterns
- Visual Patterns
- Audience Patterns
- Engagement Patterns
- Publishing Patterns
- Seasonal Patterns
- Behavioural Patterns
- Conversion Patterns
- Competitive Patterns

Future pattern categories may be introduced without changing the architecture.

---

## Pattern Formation

Patterns require:

- multiple observations
- multiple independent sources
- multiple time periods
- consistent behaviour
- observable evidence
- business relevance
- statistical consistency where applicable

One successful observation never creates a Pattern.

---

## Pattern Confidence

Every Pattern shall contain:

- Confidence
- Evidence Count
- Independent Sources
- Observation Window
- Stability
- Freshness

Confidence shall evolve as additional evidence becomes available.

---

## Pattern Lifecycle

```text
Candidate Pattern
    ↓
Observed Pattern
    ↓
Verified Pattern
    ↓
Business Memory Candidate
    ↓
Approved Business Memory
```

Only the CEO may approve Business Memory.

---

## Pattern Relationships

Patterns may reinforce other Patterns.

Patterns may weaken other Patterns.

Patterns may contradict previous Patterns.

Contradictory evidence shall never be discarded.

Contradictory evidence shall initiate re-evaluation.

---

## Pattern Decay

Markets evolve.

Patterns may lose relevance.

Pattern Intelligence continuously evaluates:

- freshness
- continued evidence
- market change
- behavioural drift
- obsolete assumptions

Historical Patterns remain preserved for traceability.

---

## Cross-Market Validation

Patterns become stronger when independently observed across multiple market environments.

### Example

A behavioural pattern observed on:

- Pinterest
- Google
- Amazon
- public websites

provides stronger evidence than a pattern observed on a single platform.

Platform independence increases confidence.

---

## Pattern Quality

Pattern Intelligence evaluates:

- repeatability
- predictive value
- business relevance
- market stability
- competitive uniqueness
- implementation feasibility
- CEO strategic relevance

---

## Pattern Repository

Verified Patterns become part of the Pattern Repository.

The Pattern Repository remains separate from:

- Business Memory
- Knowledge Library
- Operational History

The repository stores verified market behaviour.

Not business truth.

---

## Business Memory Promotion

A Verified Pattern may become a Business Memory Candidate only when:

- supported by sufficient evidence
- validated
- considered strategically valuable
- approved through the Business Memory process

Promotion is never automatic.

---

## Architectural Restrictions

Pattern Intelligence shall never:

- create business rules
- approve strategic decisions
- publish recommendations directly
- modify Business Memory
- rewrite historical observations
- infer unsupported conclusions

Evidence always precedes interpretation.

---

## Core Philosophy

Patterns are not discovered because they are interesting.

Patterns are discovered because they repeatedly explain successful market behaviour and improve future business decisions.
